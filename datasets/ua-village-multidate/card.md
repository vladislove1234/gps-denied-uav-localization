# ua-village-multidate — dataset card

> Equipment, not an experiment. There is no bar to miss here — this either
> exists and is sound, or it does not. Written by hand; `manifest.json`
> alongside it is generated by `build.py` and holds the numbers.

## What this is

Mapped Ukrainian village-and-field ground at 0.386 m/px, photographed on several
different dates, with one OpenStreetMap class mask per location. A **location**
is a 1284 × 988 m box centred on a village node. A **sample** is one location on
one date: the same ground, the same label, a different-looking picture.

The point is the *pairs*. Two dates of one location share a label and, after
co-registration, share pixels — so a model can be trained to produce the same
output for a hazy 2018 frame and a full-foliage 2025 one. That invariance is the
thing the localization system actually needs: a drone frame and a years-stale
satellite tile of the same ground must encode alike.

`crops.py` cuts this into training windows. At 256 px a crop is 99 m on the
ground, which is the 2025 camera's footprint at roughly 100 m altitude — one
crop is about one drone frame.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|
| Derived from | Esri World Imagery + Esri Wayback archive (XYZ, Web Mercator z18); OpenStreetMap via Overpass |
| Site / coverage | Rural west and centre of Ukraine — Lviv, Ternopil, Ivano-Frankivsk, Khmelnytskyi, Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Volyn. Villages ≥ 12 km apart |
| Capture date(s) | Whatever Esri's archive holds per location, typically 2018–2026, plus current |
| Sample = | one location × one date: a JPEG mosaic + the shared `label.png` |
| Split | **by location**, never by crop — `crops.py:split()`. Crops inside one village overlap and share imagery |

**This is not the flight area, deliberately.** Every drone frame in
`image_pairs_v2` sits over unmapped field: 0 of 4,181 positions have a mapped
feature in frame, nearest building 129 m away. Training ground and flight ground
are decoupled because the invariance being learned is not a property of one
village. The cost is that transfer to the drone domain is assumed here and has
to be measured elsewhere — see **Known problems**.

## How it was built

1. Every `place=village|hamlet` node in the region, filtered to Ukraine by
   ray-casting against the national boundary relation.
2. Greedy spread so no two locations are within 12 km.
3. OSM first — one Overpass query per location. Reject if it labels under 2.5%
   of pixels or yields under 1,500 building-outline pixels. This runs before any
   imagery is fetched, so unmapped ground costs one query, not a thousand tiles.
4. Wayback releases deduplicated by tile hash, then screened for *appearance* on
   a 2 × 2-tile probe at the centre.
5. Full mosaics fetched for the surviving dates, plus current World Imagery.
6. Every date co-registered to the newest by sub-pixel phase correlation.
7. Dates deduplicated again on the full mosaic by correlation < 0.97.
8. OSM rasterised once per location through the tile grid's closed-form
   georeference.

Parameters live in `manifest.json` under `config`.

## Why z18 and not z19

Wayback's archive is shallower than current World Imagery: archived releases
only hold tiles at the zoom that release actually had imagery for. Screened over
150 villages against 17 spread releases — **z18 present for 150/150 (97% with
≥ 4 releases); z19 for 3/150**. Buying 0.193 m/px would have cost the multi-date
signal that is the entire reason this dataset exists. At 0.386 m/px a 10 m house
is 26 px and a 6 m lane is 16 px.

The PoC that preceded this ran at z19 over Davydiv and passed, which was
misleading: Davydiv is near Lviv and does have a z19 archive. See `bug-012`.

## Acceptance checks

Recorded in `manifest.json` under `checks`.

| Check | Threshold | Result | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locations built | 60 target | **59** | pass |
| Distinct looks per location | ≥ 2 | **2 median, 2 min** | pass |
| Appearance correlation between kept dates | < 0.97 | **0.50 median, 0.002 min** | pass |
| Cross-date co-registration residual | ≤ 0.5 m max | **0.015 m median, 4.16 m max** | **miss** |
| OSM label offset vs imagery | ≤ 2 m median | **2.84 m median** (2.49 m over the 41 with a strong peak) | **miss** |

**Two checks missed their stated thresholds. The thresholds were not moved.**

*Label offset.* 2.84 m median against a 2 m bar, and the spread is what matters
more than the median: 21 of 59 locations are within 2 m, 15 are worse than 5 m.
At 0.386 m/px, 5 m is 13 px of label error on a 256 px crop. This is why the
check is recorded **per location** — `crops.py` excludes locations over 4 m or
with a label-correlation `peak_z` below 4, which drops 24 of 59 and leaves 35.
The bytes for the excluded ones are kept; the threshold is a training decision,
not a build decision.

*Co-registration.* The median is 1.5 cm — the dates are essentially perfectly
aligned — but the max is 4.16 m. Those are real misregistrations of older Esri
releases, with phase-correlation peak ratios of 40–75, consistent with the
3.1 m eastward offset the PoC measured at Davydiv. Two further dates scored
16.8 m and 15.7 m at peak ratios of 11.7 and 7.7 against a median of 52.9;
those were spurious peaks near the search limit, and both locations were removed
from the dataset. `build.py` now rejects any shift with a peak ratio under 15 or
a magnitude over 30 px.

## Choosing crops: boundaries, not area

`crops.py` selects windows by **class-boundary density and structural-class
presence**, not by labelled fraction. Selecting on labelled fraction is the
obvious thing and it is wrong: `wood` and `water` cover the most ground, so a
99 m crop inside a forest polygon passes a "2% labelled" test at 89% labelled
while being a uniform green mask with nothing to correlate against. The first
sampler did exactly that — see `sample-crops.png` for what the corrected one
selects. It is the same conclusion the earlier PoC reached about farmland
(*segment boundaries, not regions*), waiting one level down.

With the default filters: **35 locations, 23,726 crops, 30,741 same-window
date pairs**, split 28/7 by location.

## Known problems

**OSM describes today; the imagery may be from 2018.** A house built in 2021 is
labelled in every date, including the ones predating it. Preferring recent
releases limits this but does not remove it. This is real label noise and it is
worse on the oldest dates.

**Road widths are guesses, and this is now the dominant label error.**
`osm.ROAD_WIDTH_M` buffers centrelines by assumed widths. In `sample-crops.png`
the road band is visibly two to three times the width of the actual track and in
places covers a treeline instead of the road. Roads are a structural class and
`crops.py` selects for them, so this error is concentrated in exactly the crops
the dataset keeps. Tuning these widths is the highest-value next fix.

**Labels are sparse.** Around 5% of pixels carry a class; the rest is
unmapped field, which is correct — rural Ukraine is mostly unmapped — but means
a naive per-pixel loss is dominated by background. `crops.py` drops windows
under a labelled-fraction floor for this reason.

**A single Wayback "date" can be a mosaic of captures.** Seams are visible
within one image, so "appearance" is not uniform across a location.

**Transfer to drone imagery is untested.** Every date here is satellite or
aerial. None looks like a 1080p rolling-shutter camera at 100 m. This dataset
buys invariance *within* the satellite domain; that it transfers is a bet, and
measuring it needs the 2025 drone frames.

**No field-boundary labels yet.** Delineate Anything v2 traces the strip parcels
that dominate this terrain, and the earlier PoC found it complementary to OSM —
OSM covers the built-up strip, DA covers the fields. It is not in this build
because its failure modes (over-segmenting built-up areas, tangling under tree
cover) need a rectangularity/size filter first.
