# village-crossdate-frames — dataset card

> Equipment, not an experiment. `manifest.json` alongside it is generated by
> `build.ipynb` and holds the numbers.

## What this is

Simulated drone frames with **exact** ground truth. Each frame is the newest Esri
date (`current`) of a village, cropped heading-up at a random position and random
heading. The footprint is what a 60° camera sees from 40 m (46 × 26 m, 120 × 68 px
at ~0.38 m/px). Each frame's `meta.json` names the map to match it against: the
**oldest** Esri date of the same village. `load_frames()` works as usual.

## Source

| | |
|---|---|
| Derived from | `ua-village-multidate` (dates co-registered to 1.5 cm median) |
| Site / coverage | the segmentation models' satellite **val** ground: bushcha (whole village), birky strip (rows 0–704) |
| Capture date(s) | drone view: `current`; map: bushcha 2024-06-06, birky 2018-03-28 |
| Sample = | one crop + its pose |
| Split | all test. Neither round 3 nor MobileNet LR-ASPP trained on this ground |

## Acceptance checks

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| frames | 40 (20 per place) |
| birky frames and their whole search patch inside rows 0–704 | by construction (80 m margin) |
| drone view and map agree at the same pose | visually on bushcha in `preview.png`; birky's 2018 map looks very different (7 years, March) |

## Known problems

- Not drone imagery: coarse, perfectly nadir, no perspective.
- The birky strip is ~270 m tall, so its 20 frames overlap.
- Random positions: some frames hold no building, track, woody or water at all.
- Two places only, with very different date gaps (2 vs 7 years).

## What this is NOT valid for

Anything about the drone-to-satellite gap, drone resolution, or altitude effects.
